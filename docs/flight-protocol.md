# Flight protocol

The parabolic-flight test procedure for the Flower Low SpaceCup, flown during the Asclepios VI mission's parabolic-flight campaign. Rewritten from the mission's dress-rehearsal training slides and from Keller, K. (2026). *AMMITY*. MSc thesis, ETH Zurich, Global Health Engineering. Not published with this repository. Principal investigators: Kim Keller and Marion Dugué.

## 1. Purpose

To perform a first, qualitative test of the Flower mechanism's insertion, activation and extraction in reduced gravity, and to observe the behaviour of a menstrual-fluid analogue in and around the SpaceCup and a commercial tampon for comparison.

## 2. Equipment

- 1 glovebox, to contain the test fluid and prevent any free-floating liquid in the cabin.
- 1 GoPro, to record the procedure.
- 2 SpaceCups (Flower Low).
- 2 tampons.
- 2 artificial vaginas (AV1 and AV2).
- Syringes: 4 small (20 mL) and 1 large (60 mL).
- The test fluid, prepared no more than 2 days before the flight and refrigerated until use.

## 3. The fluid recipe

Egg white 30 mL, ketchup 50 mL, sucrose solution (30 % sugar in water) 20 mL. Ketchup gives the base viscosity and shear-thinning behaviour, egg white gives a clot-like texture, and the sucrose solution adjusts the density; the mixture was stirred gently after combining the ingredients and kept a slightly clot-like texture (thesis p.15).

## 4. Sequence over 6 parabolas

Four small-syringe injections of 6 mL each, plus a final large-syringe injection of 42 mL, for 66 mL of test fluid in total across the flight:

1. **First parabola:** get adjusted to the flight phases. In the horizontal phase, inject 6 mL of test fluid into AV1.
2. **Second parabola:** in reduced gravity, close SpaceCup 1. In the horizontal phase, extract SpaceCup 1, store it in a ziplock bag, insert SpaceCup 2 into AV1, and inject a further 6 mL into AV1.
3. **Third parabola:** in reduced gravity, close SpaceCup 2. In the horizontal phase, extract SpaceCup 2, store it, set AV1 aside, take AV2 in hand, and inject 6 mL into AV2.
4. **Fourth parabola:** in reduced gravity, extract tampon 1 (from AV2). In the horizontal phase, store tampon 1, insert tampon 2 into AV2, and inject 6 mL into AV2.
5. **Fifth parabola:** in reduced gravity, extract tampon 2. In the horizontal phase, store tampon 2, and take up the large syringe (42 mL).
6. **Sixth parabola:** in reduced gravity, inject 42 mL into AV2 (a near-complete cycle) and observe fluid behaviour and flow dynamics. In the horizontal phase, seal both artificial vaginas in ziplock bags.

Closing the SpaceCup happens during the reduced-gravity phase of a parabola; extracting it happens once the aircraft has returned to level, horizontal flight.

**Reduced-gravity window:** the thesis reports approximately 6 seconds of reduced gravity per parabola (thesis p.17); the mission's own dress-rehearsal training material gives approximately 10 seconds as the time constraint to plan around. Both figures are given here because the two sources disagree.

## 5. Roles and timing

One trained operator performed the procedure inside the glovebox for a given run, with a second trained operator ready to take over if the first became disoriented or experienced motion sickness. The GoPro recorded throughout. The procedure was trained and rehearsed on the ground beforehand, with key steps prioritised and non-critical actions skipped if a parabola's reduced-gravity window ran short.

## 6. Observations to record

Fluid movement and behaviour in reduced gravity, containment within the cup, and the performance of the sealing mechanism during activation and extraction; the same observations were recorded for the tampon runs as a comparison. Analogue astronauts also completed a post-flight questionnaire, summarised in [`validation.md`](validation.md) and [`../data/flight-questionnaire.csv`](../data/flight-questionnaire.csv).

A photo of the glovebox setup is a supplied photo, to come.

## 7. Lessons from the flights

From the thesis's discussion of the flight results (thesis p.22) and from the questionnaire notes ([`../data/flight-questionnaire.csv`](../data/flight-questionnaire.csv)):

- Glove sizes: gloves were reported too large for secure handling of the small parts.
- The fluid volume used was too small to reliably compare fluid behaviour or containment between the SpaceCup and the tampon.
- One artificial vagina leaked before its parabola began, so containment and fluid behaviour could not be observed for that run.
- Condensation formed inside the glovebox on a later flight, making the fluid hard to see.

## Safety notes

All fluid handling was confined to the glovebox; the experiment was to stop immediately on any leakage, with affected components sealed. Mechanisms were not to be forced; if a component failed, the operator was to stop and proceed to the next step.
